Deductible expenses for freelance translators in Spain: what you can deduct and what you cannot

Scope

This guide is for freelance translators with their tax residence in Spain who calculate their business income under direct assessment (estimación directa), normal or simplified. It sets out what the Income Tax (IRPF) Act and Regulation, the Corporate Tax Act, the VAT Act and the Tax Agency's practical Income Tax manual say, following their wording and with the source behind each statement. It is not tax advice: in a doubtful case, ask your accountant or the Tax Agency itself.

1. The requirements for any expense

Net income from business activities is determined under the Corporate Tax rules, without prejudice to the special rules of the IRPF Act (art. 28.1). According to the Tax Agency's practical Income Tax manual, expenses that meet these requirements are deductible:

  • They are linked to the business: they belong to it; those shown to have been incurred in carrying on the business and to be related to obtaining the income are deductible.
  • They are properly supported, primarily by the invoice from the business or professional who carried out the operation. Even so, the invoice is not privileged proof: if the Tax Agency questions on reasonable grounds whether the operations really took place, it is up to you to provide evidence that they did.
  • They are recorded in the accounts or in the record books you are required to keep.

And only expenses incurred after registering and starting the business are deductible (Income Tax manual).

2. What the law or the Income Tax manual names expressly

  • Your self-employed contribution. The manual includes among the business owner's expenses their contributions to the self-employed social security scheme (RETA). If the adjustment of those contributions in the following year results in an extra amount to pay, it is an additional deductible expense in that following year; if it results in a refund, it reduces the deductible expense in that year, and if it exceeds the contributions paid, the excess is additional income (Income Tax manual).
  • Health insurance: the premiums you pay for your own cover and that of your spouse and children under 25 living with you, up to €500 per person or €1,500 for each person with a disability (IRPF Act, art. 30.2.5.a).
  • Your own subsistence expenses incurred in the course of the business, provided they are incurred in restaurants and other hospitality establishments and paid by electronic means, within the limits the Regulation sets for employees' subsistence allowances (art. 30.2.5.c). Those limits are, per day: if you do not stay overnight in a municipality other than your usual place of work and your residence, €26.67 for travel within Spain and €48.08 abroad; if you do, €53.34 and €91.35 (IRPF Regulation, art. 9).
  • Taxes: the manual gives the business activities tax (IAE) and the property tax (IBI) relating to the business as examples of deductible non-state taxes, provided they bear on the income computed, are not penalties and relate to the same year as the income. And input VAT that is not deductible in your VAT returns is a deductible expense; VAT that is deductible in them is not (Income Tax manual).

3. If you work from home

If you partly assign your main home to the business, the home's utility expenses, such as water, gas, electricity, phone and internet, are deductible at the percentage that results from applying 30% to the ratio between the square metres of the home used for the business and its total floor area, unless a higher or lower percentage is proven (IRPF Act, art. 30.2.5.b).

The manual's own example: a 40 m² room in a 100 m² main home is 40%; 30% of that is 12%; with €5,000 of utilities a year, €600 is deductible.

4. Your computer, phone and software

  • Depreciation. Amounts for the depreciation of tangible and intangible fixed assets are deductible to the extent they match their effective depreciation, and one way for it to count as effective is to apply the straight-line depreciation rates in the Corporate Tax Act's table, which has one line for "information processing equipment" and another for "computer systems and software" (art. 12.1.a). Under the simplified method, tangible fixed assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis using the simplified depreciation table approved by the Ministry of Finance (IRPF Regulation, art. 30.1).
  • Free depreciation up to €300: new tangible fixed assets with a unit value of no more than €300 can be freely depreciated, up to a limit of €25,000 for the tax period (Corporate Tax Act, art. 12.3.e). The Income Tax manual includes this case in its chapter on direct assessment.
  • Your mobile phone: the costs of a mobile line are deductible to the extent the line is used exclusively for the business; they include buying the phone itself, which is deducted through depreciation (Income Tax manual).
  • Personal use outside working hours. Assets used both for the business and for private needs are not treated as assigned to the business, unless the private use is incidental and clearly insignificant (IRPF Regulation, art. 22.2.1). Personal use of fixed assets bought and used for the business, on non-working days or hours when the business is interrupted, counts as such. This does not apply to cars and their trailers, mopeds, motorcycles, aircraft or sports or leisure boats, except in the cases listed in the same paragraph (art. 22.4).

5. Licences, dictionaries, training and associations

Trados or memoQ licences, dictionaries and terminology databases, training or a professional association's fee are subject to the requirements in section 1: a link to the business, supporting documents and a record. It is worth being able to explain and document their link to your translation work; if it is doubtful, ask before deducting it.

6. What is not deductible, or only up to a limit

  • Criminal and administrative fines and penalties, enforcement-period surcharges and the surcharge for filing late without a prior request (Corporate Tax Act, art. 15.c).
  • Donations and gifts (art. 15.e).
  • Expenses for hospitality towards clients or suppliers are not gifts, but they are deductible only up to 1% of net turnover for the tax period (art. 15.e).

7. Input VAT follows a different path

An expense being deductible for income tax does not mean its VAT is deductible in your VAT returns. The VAT Act has its own rules:

  • VAT cannot be deducted on what is not assigned, directly and exclusively, to the business. Among other things, goods or services used at the same time for the business and for private needs are not treated as assigned to it (art. 95.Uno and Dos.2).
  • The exception is capital goods used wholly or partly in the business: their VAT is deductible to the extent they are expected to be used in the business, on well-founded criteria (art. 95.Tres.1). But no item with a purchase value below 500,000 pesetas counts as a capital good (art. 108.Dos.5), which is €3,005.06 at the euro conversion rate of 166.386 pesetas (Regulation (EC) 2866/98, art. 1). A computer costing less is not a capital good for these purposes.
  • You need the invoice. Only someone who holds the document supporting their right can deduct, which for a purchase or a service is the original invoice from the seller or supplier (art. 97.Uno.1), and never for more than the VAT expressly and separately shown on it (art. 97.Tres).
  • Also when you invoice foreign clients without VAT. You can deduct VAT on what you use in operations carried out outside the territory where the tax applies that would give the right to deduct if carried out within it (art. 94.Uno.2). That is the case, for example, of a translator who invoices an agency in another country without VAT.
  • Travel, accommodation and catering: VAT on travel, hotel and restaurant services cannot be deducted, unless their amount is a deductible expense for income tax (art. 96.Uno.6).

8. If you are under simplified direct assessment

On top of the above, deductible provisions and hard-to-justify expenses are calculated together by applying 5% to net income, excluding this item, with a maximum of €2,000 a year. This does not apply if you opt for the reduction in art. 32.2.1 of the IRPF Act, which requires the conditions in its art. 32.2.2 (IRPF Regulation, arts. 26.1 and 30.2). For the 2023 tax period the percentage was 7% (IRPF Act, fifty-sixth additional provision).

What this guide does not cover

  • Objective assessment (módulos) and companies.
  • The specific rates in each depreciation table.
  • Vehicles, which have their own rules for income tax and VAT.
  • The Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla, and the regions with their own tax system (territorios forales).
