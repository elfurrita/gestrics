What to do when an agency does not pay for a translation

Scope

This guide is for freelance translators with their tax residence in Spain working for agencies or businesses, not private individuals. It sets out what the rules say about payment terms, interest and claims, following their wording and with the article behind each statement. It is not legal advice: for a large debt or a client who disputes it, see a lawyer.

1. When a payment is late

  • If no payment date or term has been set in the contract, the term is 30 calendar days after the date on which the service is provided, even if the client received the invoice earlier (Act 3/2004, art. 4.1).
  • If the law or the contract provides for an acceptance or verification procedure to check that the service conforms to the contract, that procedure cannot last more than 30 calendar days from the provision of the service, and the payment term is 30 days after the acceptance or verification (art. 4.2).
  • You must get the invoice to the client before 15 calendar days have passed from the provision of the service (art. 4.1).
  • Those terms may be extended by agreement between the parties, but in no case to more than 60 calendar days (art. 4.3).
  • The payment term covers every calendar day of the year, and any agreement that leaves periods regarded as holidays out of the count is void (art. 2.d).
  • The Act applies to payments in commercial transactions between businesses, or between businesses and public authorities (art. 3.1), and for the Act a business is any natural or legal person acting in the course of their independent economic or professional activity (art. 2.a). Transactions involving consumers are excluded (art. 3.2.a).

2. What is owed for the delay

  • Late payment interest. A client who does not pay on time is in default and owes the agreed or the statutory interest automatically, simply because the term was not met, with no notice of the due date or formal demand needed (art. 5). You are entitled to that interest if you have met your contractual and legal obligations and have not received the amount due on time, unless the client can prove they are not responsible for the delay (art. 6).
  • At what rate. The one in the contract and, if nothing was agreed, the statutory rate: the rate applied by the European Central Bank to its most recent main refinancing operation carried out before the first day of the calendar half-year, plus eight percentage points. It applies for the six months after it is set and is published every six months in the Spanish Official Gazette, the BOE (art. 7).
  • €40 for recovery costs. When the client is in default, you are entitled to charge them a fixed €40, which is added to the principal debt "in all cases and without an express request". You can also claim compensation for all duly documented recovery costs you have incurred because of their default that exceed that €40 (art. 8.1). The client does not have to pay this compensation when they are not responsible for the delay (art. 8.2).
  • They cannot be excluded by contract. Clauses or practices that exclude late payment interest or the compensation for recovery costs are void in all cases (art. 9.1).

3. Before going to court: trying to negotiate

Since 3 April 2025, in civil matters and as a general rule, for a claim to be admitted you must first have used an "appropriate dispute resolution method" (Organic Act 1/2025, art. 5.1). The Act treats the requirement as met, among other cases, when the parties negotiate directly. For it to count, the subject of the negotiation must be the same as that of the claim (art. 5.1).

  • The order for payment procedure too. Prior negotiation is required in all declaratory proceedings of Book II and in the special proceedings of Book IV of the Civil Procedure Act, except in the matters listed in the same article (art. 5.2). The order for payment procedure (proceso monitorio) is in Book IV (Title III, on order for payment and bill-of-exchange proceedings) and is not among those matters; bill-of-exchange proceedings are.
  • How to prove it. If no neutral third party took part, with any document signed by both of you recording your identities and, where applicable, the professionals or experts who advised you, the date, the subject of the dispute, the dates of any meetings held, and a formal statement that both of you took part in good faith. Failing that, with any document proving that the client received the request or invitation to negotiate or, where applicable, the proposal, on what date, and that they could access its full content (art. 10.2). So it is worth sending it by a means that leaves proof of receipt.
  • When it ends without agreement. One of the cases the Act provides for: if 30 calendar days pass from the client receiving the initial request to negotiate and the first meeting or contact aimed at an agreement does not take place or no written reply is received (art. 10.4.a). The other cases are in the same paragraph.
  • It interrupts the limitation period. A request to negotiate that properly defines its subject interrupts the limitation period from the date there is record of the attempt to deliver it to the client at the home or workplace known to you, or through the electronic means you have used in your previous dealings (art. 7.1).

It is worth including in that request the invoice number and date, the amount, the due date, the interest and the €40 already owed, and a clear invitation to pay or negotiate, with a deadline to reply.

4. The order for payment procedure

  • What it is for. Claiming payment of a money debt of any amount that is liquid, determined, due and payable. One of the ways to prove it is with invoices or other documents that, even if created by the creditor, are of the kind that usually record credits and debts in relationships like yours (Civil Procedure Act, art. 812.1). It is worth adding whatever proves the job was ordered: the purchase order, the work order or your emails with the agency.
  • Where. The Court of First Instance of the debtor's domicile or residence has exclusive jurisdiction or, if these are not known, the one of the place where the debtor can be found (art. 813).
  • How. With a petition stating the debtor's identity, your address and theirs, and the origin and amount of the debt, together with the documents that prove it. It can be completed on a printed form obtained on paper or through the courts' electronic office (art. 814.1). You need neither a court representative (procurador) nor a lawyer to file this petition (art. 814.2; arts. 23.2.1 and 31.2.1).
  • What happens next. If the documents provided amount to prima facie evidence of the debt, the court clerk (letrado de la Administración de Justicia) orders the debtor to pay within 20 days or to file a statement of opposition setting out, "in a reasoned and substantiated way", why they do not owe the amount, in whole or in part (art. 815.1). If the debtor does not comply with the payment order or does not appear, the order for payment procedure is closed and you can apply for enforcement, for which a simple request is enough (art. 816.1).
  • If they object. The matter is decided in the appropriate trial (art. 818.1). Up to €15,000 it is a verbal trial (juicio verbal) (art. 250.2), and the procedure continues that way (art. 818.2). In verbal trials for amounts up to €2,000 you need neither a lawyer nor a court representative (arts. 23.2.1 and 31.2.1); above that, you do (arts. 23.1 and 31.1). If the claim exceeds €15,000, you have one month from being sent the statement of opposition to file a claim; if you do not, the proceedings are discontinued and you are ordered to pay the costs (art. 818.2).

5. If the agency is in another EU country

There are two European procedures for cross-border cases in civil and commercial matters, meaning cases where at least one party is domiciled or habitually resident in a Member State other than that of the court where the application is filed or that hears the case (Regulation 1896/2006, art. 3.1; Regulation 861/2007, art. 3.1):

  • European order for payment procedure: for the collection of pecuniary claims for a specific amount that are due and payable when the application is filed (Regulation 1896/2006, art. 4). The application is made on form A in its Annex I (art. 7.1), and representation by a lawyer is not required to make it (art. 24.a). The debtor can object within 30 days of being served with the order (art. 16.2). For this regulation, Denmark is not a Member State (art. 2.3).
  • European Small Claims Procedure: when the value of the claim does not exceed €5,000 at the time the court receives the claim form, excluding interest, expenses and costs (Regulation 861/2007, art. 2.1). It is started with standard claim form A (art. 4.1), it is a written procedure, and the court only holds an oral hearing if it considers it cannot give judgment on the written evidence or if a party asks for one, a request it may refuse (art. 5), and the parties are not required to be represented by a lawyer (art. 10).
  • Neither requires the prior negotiation from section 3: Organic Act 1/2025 expressly states that it is not needed to file an application for a European order for payment or to start a European Small Claims Procedure (art. 5.3).

This guide does not go into which court has jurisdiction in each case: both regulations are linked in the sources.

What this guide does not cover

  • Transactions with private individuals (consumers), which fall outside Act 3/2004.
  • Clients outside the European Union.
  • Enforcing what the court decides, the debtor's insolvency proceedings and the tax treatment of an invoice that is never paid.
