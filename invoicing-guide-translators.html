How to invoice as a freelance translator in Spain, depending on where your client is

Scope

This guide is for freelance translators with their tax residence in Spain. It sets out what the rules say about invoices, VAT and IRPF withholding, following their wording and with the article behind each statement. VAT applies in the Spanish territory defined in art. 3 of the VAT Act, which excludes Ceuta and Melilla and the Canary Islands (art. 3.Uno and 3.Dos.1, letters a and b). This guide does not cover those territories. Nor does it cover self-employed social security contributions, the business activities tax (IAE) or quarterly income tax payments.

1. The obligation to invoice

  • Businesses and professionals must issue an invoice and a copy for the services they supply in the course of their activity, including those not subject to VAT and those subject but exempt, on the terms of the Invoicing Regulation and with no exceptions other than those it provides for (Royal Decree 1619/2012, art. 2.1).
  • An invoice must be issued in every case when the recipient is a business or professional acting as such, and in any other operation where the recipient requires it in order to exercise a tax right (art. 2.2.a).

2. Content of an invoice

Every invoice and its copies must contain the items in art. 6.1 of Royal Decree 1619/2012, without prejudice to any that may be mandatory for other purposes and to the option of including other wording. These are the ones that may affect a translator; the same paragraph adds others for new means of transport, invoicing by the recipient and special schemes:

  • a) Number and, where applicable, series. The numbering of invoices within each series must be consecutive. Corrective invoices must be issued in a dedicated series (art. 6.1.a, point 2).
  • b) The date of issue.
  • c) Full name or full company name of both the issuer and the recipient.
  • d) The tax identification number, assigned by the Spanish tax authority or by that of another Member State, under which the issuer carried out the operation. The recipient's tax ID is mandatory in three cases (art. 6.1.d): 1. supplies of goods sent to another Member State that are exempt under art. 25 of the VAT Act; 2. when the recipient is the person liable for the tax on the operation; and 3. when the operation is treated as carried out in the territory where the tax applies and the issuer must be regarded as established there.
  • e) Address of both the issuer and the recipient.
  • f) Description of the operations, with all the data needed to determine their taxable base and their amount, including the unit price before tax and any discount or rebate not included in that unit price.
  • g) The tax rate or rates applied, where applicable.
  • h) The tax amount charged, where applicable, shown separately.
  • i) The date on which the operations were carried out or on which, where applicable, an advance payment was received, provided it differs from the date of issue.
  • j) If the operation is exempt, a reference to the corresponding provisions of Directive 2006/112/EC or to the corresponding provisions of the VAT Act, or a statement that the operation is exempt.
  • m) If the recipient of the operation is the person liable for the tax, the wording "inversión del sujeto pasivo" (reverse charge).

3. Deadline, currency, language and record keeping

  • Deadline. Invoices are issued when the operation is carried out. When the recipient is a business or professional acting as such, before the 16th of the month following the one in which the tax fell due (art. 11.1). For services, VAT falls due, with the exceptions set out in the same paragraph, when the taxable operations are provided, performed or carried out (Act 37/1992, art. 75.Uno.2).
  • Currency. Amounts may be expressed in any currency, provided the amount of tax charged is expressed in euros, using the exchange rate in art. 79.Once of the VAT Act (art. 12.1).
  • Language. Invoices may be issued in any language. However, when it considers it necessary to check a tax position, the tax authority may require a translation into Spanish or another official language of Spain of invoices issued in a non-official language that relate to operations carried out in the territory where VAT applies, and of those received by businesses or professionals established there (art. 12.2).
  • Record keeping. You must keep, for the period set by the General Tax Act, the invoices you receive and the copies or stubs of the ones you issue (art. 19.1), with their original content and in an orderly way (art. 19.2).

4. VAT: where the service is treated as supplied

Act 37/1992 (the VAT Act) makes subject to the tax the services supplied within its territorial scope by businesses or professionals for consideration, on a regular or occasional basis, in the course of their activity (art. 4.Uno). To know whether a service is supplied within that scope, art. 69 sets the general rules, without prejudice to those in arts. 70 and 72:

  • Recipient is a business or professional: the service is treated as supplied in Spain when the recipient acts as such and has in Spain its seat of economic activity, or a permanent establishment or, failing that, its domicile or habitual residence, provided the service is intended for that seat, establishment, domicile or residence, regardless of where the supplier is established (art. 69.Uno.1).
  • Recipient is neither a business nor a professional: the service is treated as supplied in Spain when the supplier is a business or professional and has in Spain its seat of economic activity or the permanent establishment from which it supplies the service or, failing that, its domicile or habitual residence (art. 69.Uno.2).
  • Express exception: translation, proofreading or typesetting of texts, and services provided by interpreters are not treated as supplied in Spain when the recipient is not a business or professional acting as such and is established or has its domicile or habitual residence outside the Community, unless it is established or has its domicile or habitual residence in the Canary Islands, Ceuta or Melilla (art. 69.Dos.f).
  • VAT is charged at 21%, except as provided in art. 91, which this guide does not cover (art. 90.Uno).

For a translator established in Spain, those general rules give this result:

A business or professional in SpainYesart. 69.Uno.1
A private individual in SpainYesart. 69.Uno.2
A business or professional in another EU Member StateNoart. 69.Uno.1 only places the service in Spain when the recipient is established in Spain

Spanish VAT only applies to what is treated as supplied within its territorial scope (art. 4.Uno).

5. Personal income tax (IRPF) withholding

  • Who must withhold. Under art. 76.1 of the Personal Income Tax Regulation (Royal Decree 439/2007), as a general rule the following must withhold, when they pay income subject to withholding: a) legal entities and other entities, including owners' associations and entities under the income attribution regime; b) taxpayers who carry on economic activities, when they pay income in the course of those activities; c) individuals, legal entities and other entities not resident in Spain that operate there through a permanent establishment; and d) those that operate there without a permanent establishment, in respect of the employment income they pay and of other income subject to withholding that is a deductible expense for obtaining the income referred to in art. 24.2 of the Non-Resident Income Tax Act.
  • Rate. When the income is consideration for a professional activity, the rate is 15% of the gross income paid (art. 95.1).
  • Starting out. Someone who starts carrying on professional activities has a rate of 7% in the tax period in which they start and the following two, provided they carried on no professional activity in the year before the start date. To apply it, they must tell the payer, who must keep the duly signed notice (art. 95.1).
  • What counts as professional activity. In general, that arising from the activities included in Sections Two and Three of the IAE tariffs (art. 95.2.a). In particular, art. 95.2.b.1 treats as professional income that obtained by authors or translators of works arising from intellectual or industrial property; when authors or translators publish their works themselves, their income is from business activities.

6. Business clients in another Member State: register and return

  • Register of Intra-Community Operators (ROI). It comprises those who have been assigned the VAT identification number in art. 25 of Royal Decree 1065/2007 and fall under one of the cases in its art. 3.3. Two of them affect a translator: d) businesses or professionals who supply services that, under the place-of-supply rules, are treated as supplied in another Member State when the recipient is the person liable for the tax; and c) those who receive services from businesses or professionals not established in the territory where VAT applies, for which they are the persons liable for the tax. The census registration declaration serves, among other purposes, to apply for inclusion in that register (art. 9.3.g).
  • Recapitulative statement. It must be filed by businesses and professionals who make "intra-Community supplies of services" (art. 79.1.3 of Royal Decree 1624/1992), which are those that meet all of the following: a) under the place-of-supply rules, they are not treated as supplied in the territory where the tax applies; b) they are subject to and not exempt from tax in another Member State; c) their recipient is a business or professional acting as such with its seat of economic activity, a permanent establishment or, failing that, its domicile or habitual residence in that Member State, or is a legal person that does not act as a business or professional but has been assigned a VAT identification number by that Member State; and d) that recipient is the person liable for the tax.
  • Content. The identification data of the recipients of the services and the total taxable base of the operations carried out with each of them. If the consideration was set in a currency other than the euro, the taxable base is shown in euros with reference to the date the tax fell due (art. 80.1.1).
  • Deadlines. As a general rule, for each calendar month, during the first twenty calendar days of the following month, except July's, which may be filed during August and the first twenty calendar days of September (art. 81.2.1). It is quarterly, during the first twenty calendar days of the month after the quarter, when neither in the quarter concerned nor in any of the four previous calendar quarters did the accumulated total of supplies of goods and intra-Community supplies of services to be declared exceed €50,000, excluding VAT; if it is exceeded at the end of any month in the quarter, it must be filed for the month or months elapsed since the start of the quarter (art. 81.2.2). The one for the last period of the year is filed during the first thirty calendar days of January (art. 81.3).

What this guide does not cover

  • European Directive wording. This guide does not cover any wording that Directive 2006/112/EC may require, or CJEU case law. The Directive can be consulted on EUR-Lex.
  • Invoicing with computer systems. Invoices issued with the computer systems referred to in art. 6.5 of Royal Decree 1619/2012 must also include a QR code and, only when the system sends all invoicing records to the Tax Agency, the phrase "Factura verificable en la sede electrónica de la AEAT" or "VERI*FACTU". This is not developed here.
  • The special place-of-supply rules in arts. 70 and 72 of the VAT Act, which art. 69 leaves unaffected.
  • Special schemes (equivalence surcharge, cash accounting, etc.), simplified invoices, exemptions and reduced rates.
  • Which IAE heading corresponds to your activity, on which it generally depends whether your activity is professional for withholding purposes (art. 95.2.a).
  • Whether a client not resident in Spain has to withhold tax from you. Art. 76.1 of the IRPF Regulation deals with non-residents in its letters c and d, and this guide does not go into it.
  • Other tax obligations that depend on your situation.

This guide is not tax advice and no adviser has reviewed it. If in doubt, ask an accountant.
