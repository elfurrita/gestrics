Changelog

Changes you can see when using Gestrics, version by version. Anything invisible in day-to-day use — refactors, tests, build tweaks — is left out.

This file follows Keep a Changelog and Semantic Versioning.

1.1.2 — 2026-10-28

Added

  • Metrics now tells you what each client really pays per hour. The per-word rate is what you agree on, not what you take home: an agency at €0.11/word with awkward material and three rounds of revision can pay you half per hour than one at €0.09 with clean text. Below the average rate by client there is now a table with the hours spent and the income per hour for each one, and a card at the top with the overall figure — which also says how many jobs it is talking about, because the timer gets used when you remember to. It only counts jobs already delivered, invoiced or paid (a quote is not money), and it counts the taxable base, not the total with taxes. Different currencies are never mixed into one figure. Rows are ordered by hours tracked rather than by the figure itself: with a timer it is easy to end up with a €500 job and three minutes logged, and ordering by income per hour would put that one —€10,000/h— at the top of the list.
  • The timer, on every job. It used to appear only on jobs charged by the hour, where time decides the amount. That left exactly the per-word jobs —nearly all of them— with no way to measure, and without them there is no way to know what an hour is worth. Tracking time on a per-word job does not change its price or turn it into an hourly job: the time is simply recorded, and what the hour is worth is worked out in Metrics. That is also why it is not frozen when you invoice: if on Tuesday you remember Friday's two hours, you can still log them, and that client's income per hour stays right. Not so on jobs charged by the hour: there the time is the amount, and the invoice is already out.
  • The contact search now looks inside your notes too. A note you cannot find is almost a note you never wrote: "pays at 60 days" or "the contact is Marta, extension 204" are no use six months later if you have to open the records one by one. The search already covered name, company, email, country, specialty and language pair; it now covers what you jotted down as well, ignoring accents and case as everywhere else. The subject line of sent emails is deliberately left out: it comes from the template and is identical across the whole list, so searching by it would return everything.
  • A contact's history takes dated notes, and stops being just a send log. The app knew what goes out —emails, reminders, quotes, deliveries— and nothing about what happens next: a phone call, what the agency told you, that they asked for rates. That ended up, at best, in the contact's "Notes" field, a loose piece of text with no date where the new overwrites the old. The History tab now has a field to jot things down, and every note carries its date and time and sits among the sends on the same timeline. A send shows its subject, and when it does not fit —a quote's subject ends in the amount— you can read it by hovering over it. A note you have just jotted down always lands in view, even if you were reading something from months back. Notes can be deleted, with a confirmation that shows you which one (a record with several notes has several little crosses in a row, and there is no undo); sends cannot, because they are the record of something the other side already has. Adding a note does not bring the follow-up or re-engagement alerts forward or push them back: those still count from the last email sent. And changing the contact's email address, which clears the sends and puts the record back to Pending, keeps the notes: "pays at 60 days" is about the agency and stays true with someone else at the other end — and so are quotes, deliveries and payment reminders, which are the trace of a real job, not prospecting. Nothing is reset for anyone who replied, is negotiating or is already a client: the record no longer goes back to Pending so the next "Send to pending" can cold-email them with a quote already on the table.
  • A new invoice suggests the terms of the last one to the same client. The invoice dialog always opened with taxes at 0 and the due date 30 days out: for an agency that pays at 60 days the date had to be fixed on every invoice, and if forgotten, the payment alert fired while the client was still within terms. It now suggests the rates and payment days of the last invoice to that company (even if another project manager ordered it) and says so in one line; only if it was issued under today's tax profile. Without a previous invoice, nothing changes.
  • Expenses carry the supplier's invoice. For the quarterly tax return, the accountant needs each incoming invoice's supplier, tax ID, invoice number, taxable base and input tax, and the app only stored the total. Each expense now takes, optionally, the tax included (named by the tax profile: VAT, GST...), the supplier, their tax ID and their invoice number; the resulting base shows as you type, and a tax equal to or above the total is refused. The expense export (CSV and Excel) adds those columns, with base, tax and total apart. An expense with no tax entered exports base and tax blank, not zero: for one that truly has no tax, such as the self-employed social security fee, enter 0.
  • Clients first when choosing a project's client. The dropdown listed the whole contact book from newest to oldest, with the person's name first: after importing 300 agencies to prospect, the two real clients were at the very bottom, and typing "Reed" did not reach "Tom Reed · Reed Legal". Clients (with any project, or marked as active client) now have their own group at the top, with every other contact below; each group is alphabetical, with the company first.
  • Time of day on delivery deadlines. Agencies give deadlines with a time ("Monday at 9:00"), and the app only stored the day: at 11 that Monday it still said "Due today". A project now takes an optional time next to the delivery date (typed as it comes: "9", "9:00", "17h30"). Once that time passes, the project page, the notification bell and the calendar show it as overdue ("Was due today at 09:00"), and the calendar shows the time before the title. Alerts now refresh on their own while the app stays open, at that time, at midnight and when you return to the window (with a laptop asleep overnight, midnight arrived late): before, they were only worked out on launch. The same goes for the stale-backup notice.
  • One reminder for all of a client's overdue invoices. Agencies are often late with several at once, and the project manager got one email per invoice on the same day. The reminder for one invoice now lists the client's other overdue invoices, with amount, due date and the total outstanding, and once sent they are all marked as reminded: the bell no longer asks for one email each. If any of them carries the previous bank account, the reminder gives the new one even when the main invoice already shows the current one.
  • Notice when charging VAT where it does not belong. Charging 21% out of habit to a German agency is a common mistake: a business in another EU country is invoiced without VAT, under the reverse charge, and a client outside the EU is not charged it either. The issue dialog now says so in those two cases, following the same rule that decides the 0% wording on the invoice; it does not warn for a private client in the EU (no tax ID), where VAT does apply.
  • Backup notice in the bell. If there is no backup or the last one is a week old or more, the bell says so ("No backups" / "Last backup 12 days ago") and takes you to Settings, where it is downloaded. Before, it only showed inside Settings, which is not opened day to day, and the app holds invoices that must be kept.
  • Billing email on the contact record. Many agencies ask for invoices to go to their accounts department rather than to the project manager who places the order. If the contact has a "Billing email", the invoice and the payment reminders go there; quotes and deliveries still go to their usual email. It is optional, validated as a single address, and carried by the backup and by contact export and import.
  • BIC/SWIFT notice when invoicing a client outside the EU. A client in the United States or the United Kingdom pays by international transfer, which needs the BIC as well as the IBAN. The invoice already printed it when it was in the profile; now, if it is missing, the issue dialog says so. It does not warn when the client is in the translator's own country, where the transfer is domestic.
  • Restore a backup from the welcome screen. Someone setting up a new computer no longer has to fill in their profile and email only for the restore to overwrite them: the first screen of the setup wizard offers "Moving from another computer? Restore a backup", and once it finishes the app starts with their data, without going back to the wizard.
  • Fixed price. A new project type for work not charged by the word or the hour: a sworn translation per document, a minimum fee, a flat-rate job. You enter the price, and the quote and the invoice show it as a single line ("Translation EN-ES — fixed price", 1 × price), with no word count or per-word rate.
  • Warning for a very high per-word rate. Typing 0.85 instead of 0.085 multiplied the amount by ten with nothing to say so. From 0.50 per word in euros, dollars, pounds and similar currencies, the form now shows a one-line warning ("check the decimals"); it does not block.
  • Accounting export also in Excel. CSV is the format Holded, QuickBooks or Xero ask for, but opened with a double click in Excel set to Spanish it put everything in the first column, with broken accents and no amount that could be added up. Invoices and expenses can now also be exported as .xlsx, with amounts as real numbers.
  • A warning when issuing an invoice without VAT to a client in Spain. The dialog opens with the tax at 0, which is right for a foreign client, and an invoice to a Spanish company could be issued like that with nothing to flag it. It is a warning, not a block: there are exceptions the app cannot know.
  • A warning when issuing without VAT to a client in your own country without having written the reason in the VAT note of your profile (outside Spain, where the warning above applies). This is the case for anyone exempt, such as a French micro-entrepreneur under the franchise scheme: the invoice has to say why there is no VAT.
  • 7% IRPF among the one-click rates, next to 0% and 15%: it is the rate for those starting out, and until now it had to be typed in.
  • Marking "Paid" asks for the payment date, with today suggested. It used to store the date of the click, and since you mark it whenever you remember, the average payment time in Metrics came out wrong. Future dates are not accepted.
  • When creating a first project with no clients yet, the form says where to add them. The drop-down used to come up empty with no explanation.
  • The project's purchase order (PO) number is printed on the invoice, under the due date. Agencies ask for it to match the invoice to their order.
  • A warning when sending the quote for an hourly project with no hours logged, which would come out at 0.00.
  • Correcting an invoice's amount, or cancelling it. Until now an issued invoice could only have its VAT and withholding corrected: an invoice for 5,000 words instead of 3,000, a discount agreed afterwards or a cancelled job had no way out. Editing the invoice now lets you correct the words, hours, rate or price, or tick "Void the invoice" (base to 0). It produces an R- corrective invoice with the corrected line, the reason and the amount corrected, and the project card is brought up to date. A voided invoice no longer shows as overdue.
  • Estimated hours on hourly projects. Hours only came from time tracking, so hourly work could not be quoted before starting: the quote showed 0 h and 0.00. A project now takes estimated hours, which the quote, its email and its PDF use ("Estimated hours: 10.00 h", €500.00). The invoice still charges the hours actually logged.

Changed

  • For someone who already replied, "Send now" offers the first follow-up. With a contact that had responded, was in negotiation or was an active client, the editor opened the prospecting template, and "Send now" offered the introduction email to someone you had already been talking to. It now opens the first follow-up; if something in the cadence is due, it still opens that, and for everyone else it is prospecting as before.
  • The batch says it sends the templates as they were when it started. Fixing a typo mid-batch and seeing "Saved" suggested the rest would go out fixed, and it didn't: what you confirmed goes out. It now says so under the progress bar; to send the new version, cancel and start it again.
  • The second follow-up and re-engagement no longer repeat emails. There was a single follow-up template for both touches, so the same agency got the same text, word for word, at 10 days and at 20. And the 90-day re-engagement resent the original cold email to people who hadn't replied or had said no. The Template menu now has four: Prospecting, First follow-up, Second follow-up and Re-engagement, and each contact gets the one that's due, in batches too (a follow-up batch mixes first and second follow-ups, and each goes out with its own). The two new ones come with their own text: the second follow-up offers a sample and leaves the door open, and re-engagement mentions the first contact. Until you give them a subject, they use the first follow-up's and the prospecting one's, and the empty field says which, so you don't have to write two more subjects to keep sending.
  • The send question says which email goes out. With four templates, the one showing in the editor may not be the one that contact is due: changing the menu to look at another after picking them is enough. "Send now" now asks "Send “Re-engagement” to …?", and a batch says which templates it uses (a follow-up batch usually has two).
  • The history opens full height while you read it. The timeline lived in a 110-pixel window at the foot of the record: with six or seven entries you had to scroll it to read the lot, which is exactly what nobody does before calling an agency. Clicking "History" now raises the panel until it fills the whole column, and the list takes all of that space; moving the mouse off the panel puts it back, with nothing to click. Clicking "History" again opens it once more. Moving between Details and History is animated too: before, the content swapped at once and the foot jumped in height.
  • The note field now reads "Add a note", and its button says "Add". It used to read "Note a call, what they told you…" and "Add note".
  • The website opens in the app's language. With the interface in Spanish, Help › outreachstudio.app and the new-version banner's button led to the English home page, and Help › Terms of Use opened the terms in English; they now lead to the Spanish home page, which has had its own address since 2026-10-20, and to the terms in Spanish.
  • PDFs are named in English, like the document: the invoice "Invoice-2026-001.pdf", the corrective invoice "Rectificative-Invoice-R-2026-001.pdf", the quote "Quote-12.pdf". They were called "Factura-…" and "presupuesto-…", including in the attachment the client receives.
  • The invoice dialog no longer offers withholding in France, where a freelancer's invoice does not carry it. It is only removed for countries that have been checked (France, for now); elsewhere the field stays, because not having researched it does not mean it does not exist. An invoice that already has withholding still shows it when corrected.
  • The withholding field uses its name in your country: with the Mexico profile it says "ISR (%)", as the summary and the hint already did, instead of a generic "Withholding (%)".
  • The tax ID in your profile and your clients' tax IDs are written in capitals: a "12345678-z" typed that way was printed as such on the invoice.
  • Countries on the contact card in the app's language, and sorted in it: with the app in Spanish you had to look for "Alemania" under "Germany". What is stored — and printed on the invoice — is still the English name.

Fixed

  • The send questions didn't say which attachments went out. Prospecting attachments are kept and go with every send, including those days later. An invoice attached for one client and forgotten went out in the next batch to every agency, with that client's details. "Send now" and the batch now name them before you confirm.
  • "Send now" didn't warn about missing details. The batch already said "one contact has no company, their email will have gaps"; a single send didn't, and a template with {{empresa}} went out with "… for ." without a word. The question now says so.
  • A contact marked "Sent" by hand never got a follow-up. Emailing an agency from your own mailbox, or applying through its portal, and marking it "Sent" left it with no contact date: the app never asked for the follow-up, and it showed up neither in the bell nor in any batch. The same with an imported sheet that says "Sent" with no date, and with "Rejected" and re-engagement. With no other date, it now counts from when it is marked or imported.
  • Saving a contact's details could put it back to "Pending" and repeat the cold email. The "Edit" form saved the status the contact had when it was opened. With the form open to fix a phone number while a batch ran, the batch emailed the contact and Save put it back to "Pending": the next "Send to pending" sent the first email again. The form's status is now only saved if you changed it there.
  • "Send now" could send a second email to someone the batch had just emailed. With the confirmation open while the batch emailed that contact, confirming sent another email; clicking it while the batch's email was going out did the same. It now won't send while an email to them is on its way, and after you confirm it checks the contact again, as the batch does.
  • Marking a contact "Responded" while an email was going out didn't stick. Once the send finished, the contact went back to "Sent", and ten days later a follow-up was due for someone who had already replied. The status you set now stays.
  • A typo in a template made the contact forget which email was due. Sending with a placeholder that doesn't exist (a {{nmae}}) left the contact as "Send failed" without logging which touch had failed. Once the typo was fixed, the retry no longer went out as a follow-up but with the open template, which on selecting the contact again is the prospecting one: the cold email all over again. The warning still shows, but the contact now stays as it was, as it already did with an empty subject.
  • An email-account failure marked the whole batch as failed. With the app password revoked or no connection, the batch carried on and left each contact as "Send failed", one every few seconds, for something that was none of theirs. It now stops at the first account failure, leaves the contact alone and says the rest are left as they were; once the account is fixed, the next batch picks them up with their usual email.
  • Importing a list of agencies without people imported nothing. A sheet with just the agency and the email ended in "No contacts imported — could not be saved", without saying why. With no name column, the company name is now used ("Hi Lingua Partners,") and the screen says so; with neither name nor company it won't import, and a stray row with neither is counted as "with no name".
  • The change-email warning advised keeping the typo. It said that if you were only fixing a typo you should cancel and leave the address as it was. But what was sent to a misspelt address never arrived: resetting the record and sending the first email to the right address is correct, and cancelling left the broken address with follow-ups still going to it. It now says so, and the count no longer reads "its 1 logged send(s)".
  • Closing the app right after saving something lost it. The database is written to disk a second later, and closing the app stopped the internal server abruptly, before that write: a contact added half a second before closing was gone when the app reopened. If it was the last send of a batch, the next batch would have repeated it. The app now waits until everything is saved before closing.
  • Closing the app while an email was going out left it unlogged. The email went out but the record never knew, and the next batch would have sent it to that contact again. Now, if you close with an email going out, the app says so, cancels the rest of the batch, waits until that email is logged and then closes on its own. With no email going out, it closes at once.
  • "1 send left of the daily limit", not "1 sends left".
  • A batch emailed people who had changed while it was sending. The list was taken when the button was clicked and gone through as it was, for minutes. An agency marked "Responded" mid-batch still got the follow-up (and the send took away its "Responded"), and one whose email was corrected got written to at the old address. Each contact is now looked at again just before its email: if it was deleted, changed its email or is no longer due that email, it's left out and the log says so.
  • Emailing an active client put them back into prospecting. After any send the contact became "Sent", active clients and negotiations included, and ten days later the app asked you to send them a follow-up. They now keep their status, whether the send works or fails.
  • A pending contact could get something other than the first email if it had a last-contact date (a sheet can say "Pending" and carry a date). A pending contact now only ever gets the prospecting email.
  • Importing a sheet of agencies you'd already written to left them pending. The status was only recognised as the app stores it internally ("sent", "activeClient"), so a sheet using the on-screen words ("Active client", "Send failed", or the Spanish "Enviado") brought those rows in as Pending, and the next "Send to pending" repeated the cold email. The on-screen word is now recognised too, in English and Spanish, with or without accents. And a row with no status but a last-contact date comes in as Sent, so the cadence carries on from there.
  • "Send to pending" could send a follow-up as the first email. Pending contacts got whichever template was open in the editor, and that template switches on its own when you click a contact: looking at someone due for a follow-up and then clicking "Send to pending" sent the follow-up to new contacts, and logged it as one. Now anyone you've never written to only gets the prospecting email.
  • Retrying a failed follow-up sent the cold email again. When a send fails because of that email (the server rejects the address or the message), the contact is left as "Send failed", and "Send to pending" retried it with the prospecting template: the follow-up that had failed turned into the first email again. Now it retries what failed: the first email, the first follow-up, the second, or re-engagement. (Account failures, such as an expired password or a dropped connection, no longer mark the contact: see above.)
  • Tracking time on a per-word job rewrote its price. A 10,000-word job at €0.11 (€1,100.00) dropped to €3.08 as soon as 28 hours were logged against it, because the amount was recalculated as hours × rate; and the job then counted as an hourly one for the rest of the app. It could not be reached from the screen —the timer only showed on hourly jobs— but it could from the API, and now that the timer is on every job, that was the normal route.
  • Exported CSV and TSV files came out with broken accents when opened in Excel. "Agencia Niño" arrived as "Agencia NiÃ±o", and "Número factura" as "NÃºmero factura": Excel on Windows does not assume a text file is UTF-8, so it opened them with the system's own encoding. All four were affected: the contact export to CSV and to TSV, and the invoice and expense CSVs you send your accountant. All four now carry the marker Excel looks for. The Excel and OpenDocument exports did not need it, because those formats declare their encoding internally. What CSV still cannot fix is the separator: a Spanish Windows expects semicolons, so opening one by double-click still drops every column into the first — that is what the Excel export is for.
  • Typing in a template made the contact's record disappear. The moment you typed the first letter in the editor, the bar at the bottom —Details, History, the send button— left the screen entirely, and did not come back until you undid the changes or restarted the app. It was hidden on purpose, to give the editor the height needed to read what you were writing, but the editor has had a fixed height for a while now: measured, it is the same size with the bar and without it, so the whole record was being paid for nothing. And that is exactly when it is needed most: seeing the placeholders filled with real data requires a contact to be selected. What hiding it did prevent was sending an email with a half-written template —what goes out is always the saved version, not what is in the editor— and the "Send?" prompt now says so itself. Also when sending to pending contacts, which was never hidden and sent the saved version without saying a word.
  • Pressing Enter twice in a row jotted the note twice. A repeating key or a double tap created two identical history entries a millisecond apart; two quick clicks on "Add" did the same. The lock meant to prevent it did not take effect in time.
  • One broken line in a history made the app unusable. If a contact's history carried a corrupt entry —only possible by importing a sheet with the "history" column edited by hand— opening its record did not just fail in that tab: it took down the whole app with "Unexpected error". That line is now skipped and the rest of the history shows normally; the data is still stored and still travels in the backup. And deleting a healthy note that had one of those lines above it no longer fails either. The same went, and worse, for a line that was a proper entry but whose text or subject was not text: merely SELECTING the contact —without even opening the tab— was enough to lose the app. Only text is drawn now and the rest stays quiet, and an unreadable date no longer shows as "Invalid Date" twice per line. That same line also left the "Send to pending" button MUTE: the daily-limit count walks every contact's history, so one broken entry in any of them was enough for the button to do nothing at all —no batch, no warning, no error.
  • A note that failed to save looked saved. With the server down, jotting something in the history left it drawn on the timeline and cleared the field: the text vanished from the screen, the note looked saved, and only a short-lived warning said it had failed. On reload it was gone. Now, if the save fails, the entry is withdrawn and what you typed stays in the field so you can retry. This applies to any change to the record, not just notes. And withdrawing it removes only what that save had added: if you jotted another note meanwhile —or the batch sent an email— that stays put instead of vanishing from the screen along with it.
  • A batch send could undo whatever you did while it was sending. The run took a snapshot of the contacts when it started and wrote from it as each one's turn came: with 50 agencies at 8-second intervals, the last one was written with its state from nearly seven minutes earlier. Anything that had happened to it meanwhile —a note jotted down, a quote sent from Projects, a correction to its record— vanished with no error and no trace when its turn arrived. The same happened, in shorter windows, with payment reminders, deliveries and quotes. Every write is now composed against the contact as it is at that moment, not against the caller's copy.
  • A sheet with European dates shifted the cadence by months, silently. The "last contact" and "first contact" columns were imported as-is, while the rest of the app expects YYYY-MM-DD. A 05/09/2026 —5 September across much of Europe— was read as 9 May: four months out, looking like good data, with no warning at all. Those dates drive the follow-up (10 days) and re-prospecting (90 days) reminders, so the app ended up telling you to re-prospect an agency you wrote to last month. With a day above 12 (25/09/2026) the record simply read "Invalid Date". From an .xlsx the dates now come through correctly, whatever format the sheet displays them in: the file knows that cell is a date, and that is what gets used. From a CSV there is no way to tell —a date there is just text— so only YYYY-MM-DD comes in; what is ambiguous is not guessed (05/09 is 5 September here and 9 May in the United States) and the import says so when it finishes, asking for that format. No unreadable date shows as "Invalid Date" anywhere again.
  • A sheet with a "status" column left those contacts in limbo. The import normalised the country but not the status: whatever the column held went in as-is. A contact imported as "Nuevo" or "New lead" was drawn in the list as Pending —icon and all, indistinguishable from a real one—, its record read "Status: NuevoStatus", it counted in no funnel figure, it showed under no filter but "All statuses", and "Send to pending" did not pick it up. With three hundred agencies imported that way, the button still said you had one pending and the rest were never written to. Anything that is not one of the app's own statuses now comes in as Pending, and the import says so when it finishes.
  • The history called prospecting emails "Follow-up", and that switched the reminders off. The app decided twice, by different rules: which template to send went by the ten days a contact has to have gone without replying, while how to record it went by another rule —"already in Sent"— that holds from day one. So clicking "Send now" on someone you wrote to three days ago sent the cold email and recorded it as a follow-up. What you could not see is that it also raised the follow-up count, and that count is what switches the reminder off at two and opens re-prospecting at ninety days: insisting twice by hand was enough for the app to stop reminding you about the follow-up you never sent. The type now comes from the template that actually goes out, and the count only rises when what goes out is a follow-up.
  • Saving a contact's form could wipe its history. The pencil opens the form without closing the record's footer, so both are visible at once; the form kept a copy of the contact from the moment it opened and wrote the whole thing back on save. Anything recorded in the history meanwhile vanished with no warning. The form now saves only the fields it actually edits.
  • No purchased licence key could be activated. The 1.1.2 clean-up that strips the full stop or quotes picked up when copying the key from the email also stripped the "==" every key ends with, and the app reported it as non-existent. That ending is now kept, and stray punctuation is still stripped.
  • On Mac, invoices to Japanese, Chinese or Indian clients no longer show gaps in the name. For those scripts the PDF uses a system font, and on macOS the app looked for fonts that do not exist under those names: the client's name, a required part of the invoice, came out blank or partial. Checked on a GitHub-hosted Mac with clients in Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Hindi and Thai. Nothing changes on Windows.
  • The invoice to a client outside the EU with no tax ID cited the wrong article. For any client abroad, the "not subject" wording cited art. 69.Uno.1º of the VAT Act, which only holds when the recipient is a business or professional; a private individual falls under 69.Dos.f, the one that names translation. The app cannot tell whether the client is a business or a private individual, so with no client tax ID it now cites art. 69 alone, which is true in both cases. With a tax ID it still cites 69.Uno.1º.
  • A project created for the wrong client could not be fixed. Editing it offered no client field, and the only way out was to delete it, along with its logged time and deliverables. The client can now be changed when editing, as long as the project has no invoice; once invoiced it cannot, since the client is recorded on the invoice: void it with a correction and invoice the work in another project.
  • The quote hard-coded the tax names. Its note said "Excludes VAT/withholding tax" for everyone, including a translator in India (who charges GST) and one in France (who has no withholding). It now uses the names of the translator's country, like the invoice: "Excludes VAT/IRPF" in Spain, "Excludes VAT" in France, "Excludes GST" in India.
  • Project search did not find by language pair or content type. Searching "DE-ES" or "technical" to look back over a year's work gave nothing. It does now, and content type also by the name the interface shows in each language.
  • Searching contacts by country, specialty or language pair found nothing. The country is stored in English even when the record shows it in Spanish, so "alemania" gave no results; and specialty ("legal") and language pair ("DE-ES") were not searched. Fixed

Backup

  • Restoring a backup silently dropped eleven columns. The restore writes each table naming its columns one by one, and the ones added later were never added there. Among the losses: the issuer identity frozen at invoicing time — so an old invoice went back to using your CURRENT details — the tax country it was issued under, the date of supply, the payment-reminder state — your client received the whole chasing sequence again for an invoice they may already have paid — the Stripe payment link, and the flag marking a deliverable as already sent. The quietest one: the period of a recurring invoice, which is what stops the same month being invoiced twice; without it, restoring was enough to issue that month's invoice a second time. A backup now returns exactly what it stored, and a check verifies it by exporting, restoring and comparing.

Invoices and quotes

  • A recurring plan's card read "Source words 1" and "320.00 EUR/word". A monthly fee is charged neither by word nor by hour, but internally it is stored as one word at a "rate" equal to the fee, and that showed up raw on every retainer. The invoice PDF already handled it; the card now does too, showing the fee under its own label.
  • The quote no longer disappears once the job moves on. As soon as the project left "Quoted", the PDF the client is looking at could no longer be downloaded: getting it back meant moving the project to its previous state. Its download button now stays, without the edit and resend options, which no longer apply there.
  • A job could charge a cent other than the one its own lines added up to. With a CAT breakdown, the total was worked out by summing raw amounts and rounding at the end, while the PDF prints one already-rounded row per category: at a rate of €0.11, the five rows add up to €145.69 and the total read €145.70. Same for hourly work, where genuine half-cents turn up: a quarter of an hour at €32.50/h is €8.125, an amount that does not exist in money, and it was stored that way every time you stopped the timer. The total is now always the sum of what is printed, and every amount the app CALCULATES — words by rate, hours by rate, CAT breakdown — is rounded to the cent before being stored.
  • The invoice ledger filed a number its own signature did not back. The taxable base went into the hash-chained record exactly as it came out of multiplying words by rate — 1,480 × 0.14 gives 207.20000000000002 in floating point — while the hash sealing that record is computed on the rounded figure. None of this showed on screen or in the PDF, which always formatted correctly, but anyone reading the database directly — an audit, a future export — got the dirty value, and base + tax did not match the stored total.
  • A quote already sent looked exactly like one never sent. The project card still read “Send the quote to the client or edit it first” weeks after it had gone out: the only place that knew was the “Quotes” counter in the header, so it was easy to send the client the same quote twice. The card now shows the day it was sent and the button becomes “Resend quote”.
  • A newly created recurring billing plan did not appear under Alerts until the app was restarted. The dialog itself tells you generation is confirmed from Alerts; you went there following that instruction and found it empty. It now shows up — and disappears when the plan is deleted — without a restart.
  • The reason for a correction is printed on the invoice the client receives, and the help text did not say so. It only said it was “recorded alongside the correction”, which reads like an internal filing note. It now warns that it is printed on the client's document and that it should be written in English, like the rest of the invoice.
  • A recurring invoice was issued without the project or the invoice showing up anywhere. Confirming it from Alerts while already on the Projects tab, the app reported it had been generated and the list did not change: the new project and its invoice — already recorded in the ledger, with its number — were nowhere to be seen until the app was restarted. The list only reloaded when switching tabs, so it failed on exactly the likeliest path: looking at your projects and confirming the alert. It now refreshes on its own and opens the new project, which is what it meant to do all along.
  • One invoice in ten did not add up to its own total. VAT and withholding are rounded to the cent, but the total was computed on the full decimals, so the totals block could contradict itself: on a €50.03 base the invoice read 50.03 + 10.51 − 7.50 with a TOTAL of 53.03 instead of 53.04. Sweeping bases from €50 to €10,000 at the usual rates, 1,160,062 of 11,940,012 combinations failed to add up. Each amount is now rounded to its currency's smallest unit — the yen has no cents, the Kuwaiti dinar has three — and the total is the sum of those figures, which is what gets paid.
  • The CAT breakdown lines did not add up to their own total. Each line was rounded on its own while the total came from the exact amounts, so the printed lines could differ from the figure below by a cent: at €0.0333 per word, four lines added up to €43.92 while the total read €43.91. On a quote that invites a question from the client; on an invoice the mismatch was against the taxable base, which is precisely what those lines exist to determine. The difference is now allocated to the largest line and the document adds up.
  • An invoice with no issue date vanished from the accounting CSV. It was dropped even when exporting everything with no date range, so an issued invoice could simply never reach your accountant without anything saying so. It now appears in the full listing, with the date column blank so the problem is visible; a quarterly export still leaves it out, since without a date it cannot be assigned to a period.
  • A client name in Hindi (or any Indic script) came out as empty boxes. The fallback font meant to cover them was looked up under a filename that does not exist on Windows, so it was never used. It now prints correctly, and the internal warning that reported the lost characters — which did work — stops firing.
  • New warning when invoicing a client whose details are in Arabic or Hebrew. The PDF prints those letters reversed and unjoined, because it does not lay out right-to-left text. Since the recipient's name is mandatory on an invoice, the dialog now warns you before issuing instead of letting you find out in the document you already sent.
  • A country written without accents left the invoice missing its mandatory wording. The dropdown on the client card stores canonical names, but importing contacts from a spreadsheet stores whatever the file says: "Espana", "Belgica", "Paises Bajos", "Deutschland", "Holanda", "USA"… None were recognised, and an unrecognised country meant the app did not print the reverse-charge wording on an intra-EU invoice. Accents, dots and the most common endonyms now resolve alike. And when the country still isn't recognised, the issuing dialog warns before you issue instead of staying silent.
  • Editing a paid invoice wiped its payment date. Just opening the invoice and saving again was enough, even without changing anything: the dialog doesn't send the payment date, and the server blanked it anyway. From then on, the CSV for your accountant reported that invoice as unpaid with an empty payment-date column, it dropped out of "Collected" and out of the average time-to-payment in Metrics, and the job card offered to generate a payment link again for something your client had already paid.
  • The default due date counted from today, not from the invoice date. An invoice dated earlier said "30 days" and fell due after 47. The sum was also done in hours: the day daylight saving ends is 25 hours long, so the term landed a day early. It is now 30 days from the invoice date, counted in days.
  • Dates were off by one day outside the Madrid time zone. A date stored as 2026-08-20 was read as midnight UTC, so in Mexico, the United States, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Argentina or Canada it was shown and printed as the 19th. It affected everything at once: the invoice issue and due dates, the quote's validity, the date of supply, and every date in the interface. Writing failed the other way round — the UTC day was used — so invoicing in the afternoon in Mexico issued the document with tomorrow's date, and that date is frozen into the invoice ledger. Alerts were off too: an invoice showed as overdue on its own due date, and the payment reminder reached the client a day early.
  • You could issue an invoice without your own tax details. Until now the tax ID and address were optional fields in Settings, so a fresh installation could generate an invoice whose issuer was just a name. Such an invoice is not valid anywhere. The app now refuses to issue it and says exactly which fields are missing and where to fill them in; the rejected invoice consumes no number and leaves no trace. The client's details are not blocked — their tax ID is not always mandatory, and requiring it would prevent invoicing a private individual — but the issuing dialog warns when they are missing and explains when they matter.
  • Correcting an issued invoice left two documents in circulation under the same number. When you changed the VAT or withholding on an invoice you had already sent, the app asked for a reason and recorded the correction in its internal ledger, but the PDF still came out with the original number and date and the new amounts: the client ended up with two different pieces of paper both claiming to be invoice 2026-001. The QR code, which did read the right ledger entry, also contradicted the heading printed right above it. The document is now issued as what it is — "RECTIFICATIVE INVOICE #R-2026-001", with its own date, the invoice it corrects, the reason and the rectified amount — and the QR agrees with the heading. The project card and the email attachment use that number too.
  • The invoice said two different things about when it was due, and neither was yours. The top right read "Payment due: 30 days end of month" and halfway down the page "Payment due upon receipt": both were hardcoded and contradicted each other, while the due date you actually picked when issuing never appeared anywhere. The invoice now shows that real date, next to the issue date. Leave it blank and nothing is printed, rather than inventing a term.
  • "Invoice notes" did not appear on the invoice. The issuing dialog asks for that text under exactly that label and suggests using it for an account number or payment terms; it was then stored and never shown on the document. It now prints below the payment terms, on its own page if needed and without truncation — it is where a legal mention goes, such as the reverse charge on an intra-EU invoice.
  • Changing your own details rewrote invoices you had already sent. The issuer's name, tax ID, address and bank details were read from Settings every time a PDF was downloaded, not from what they were at issue time. Moving house, fixing a typo or incorporating changed who appeared as the issuer on every earlier invoice, including the ones the client already had. That identity is now frozen on the invoice, and the QR code likewise stops attributing an old invoice to a new tax ID. Invoices issued before this change behave as they did, since they have nothing frozen to show.
  • A foreign client's name was destroyed in the PDF. Both documents were generated with the PDF format's standard fonts, which only cover the Western Latin alphabet. Any other character came out as garbage, with no warning: a Polish agency called "Biuro Tłumaczeń Sp. z o.o." was printed as "Biuro T'VÖ7eB7.". The same went for Czech, Turkish, Romanian, Greek, Cyrillic, Japanese, Chinese and Korean — and the recipient's name is a mandatory invoice detail. Only Spanish accents and the euro sign were spared, which is why invoicing within Spain never showed the problem. Both documents now embed a full font, with automatic fallback for alphabets that font does not cover, and anything no installed font can represent is recorded in the application log instead of silently vanishing.

Sending email

  • The "no email account" warning could lie in both directions. The header decided whether an account existed by looking at a copy kept inside the database, while sending uses the real credentials, which live encrypted outside it. The two drifted apart: wipe the database and the warning stayed on forever with email working perfectly; and the other way round — restoring a backup, which carries the username but never the password — the app treated as configured an account every send was going to fail on. And it was not just the warning: the Outreach batch send was gated on that same copy, so with the database wiped it refused to send anything — "no email account configured" — while email worked perfectly, and after restoring a backup it started the batch only to fail contact by contact. All of that now asks the server, which is the side holding the credentials, and it also tells apart a third case that did not exist before: an account that is saved but rejected by the provider, with its own warning in the header and, in Settings, a red dot reading "Saved, but the provider rejects it" instead of the green one reassuring you about a password that does not work. The welcome wizard was taking the same wrong path: it counted an account with a saved address but no password as connected, and now only does so when the server confirms it can send.

And the invoice subject was the only one of the seven carrying no reference at all — “Invoice for Translation Services” — so three invoices to the same client arrived with identical subjects and their mail client stacked them into a single thread, which is the opposite of what chasing an unpaid invoice needs. All four now greet the client by name, and the invoice subject carries its number and the project. If you had already rewritten a template, yours is kept: this only changes the factory defaults.

The email delivering the translation says “Please find attached the completed work”, and the invoice one, “Please find attached the invoice”. If no file was added, or if the PDF was removed from the attachment list before hitting send, the email went out anyway: no warning, no way to undo it, and a client reading the promise of a file that was not enclosed. With no attachment at all, sending now stops and says which file is missing.
  • The payment reminder gave the due date in a format the client reads the other way round. These emails always go out in English, and the amount was already formatted for the client, but the date followed the interface language: “€676.00” and “10/8/2026” sat in the same sentence. That date is 10 August to whoever runs the app in Spanish and 8 October to whoever reads the email in English, so the final overdue notice ended up contradicting itself: a due date the client reads as being in the future, in an email chasing them for 39 days of delay. The month is now written out — “August 10, 2026” — which cannot be read two ways in any market. This covers all three reminder stages and the invoice email.
  • An already-saved password looked exactly like a field that never had one. The server never returns the email account password, not even encrypted, so the Settings field showed up empty whether or not an account was connected. Anyone coming back months later had no way to tell if their email was still set up. Now, when there is one, a green dot and “One is already saved” appear next to the label, and the field itself says “Leave blank to keep it unchanged”.
  • The same email could reach the client three times. If the connection dropped right after the message was handed over — the server already had it queued and only the confirmation was lost — the app treated it as failed and sent it again up to three times, then reported an error. In outreach that means the same cold email repeated to a prospect. Only failures known to have happened before delivery are retried now, such as a temporary rejection of the recipient; everything else is reported without sending again.
  • Every refusal from the mail server was announced as "check your app password". An address that does not exist, a message that is too large or one blocked by a spam filter all sent you off to retype a password that was perfectly fine, with no way out: the check in Settings kept saying it was correct. Each case now says what actually happened.
  • A server that stalled halfway could leave the app on "Sending…" for up to half an hour — ten minutes per attempt, and it tried three times — with nothing to click and no way to tell whether the email had gone out. Each stage of the connection now has its own timeout.
  • The subject line could go out with unresolved variables. If it contained "{{idiomas}}" or "{{especialidad}}" — the body template accepts both, and the unresolved-variable warning treated them as fine — the client received the literal braces in the subject line of a cold email. The contact's history even stored the correctly resolved subject, so it did not show up there either.
  • An empty attachment was sent as if nothing were wrong. A 0-byte file — an export that failed halfway, or one the cloud had not finished downloading — was accepted, listed as an attachment, and then silently dropped at send time: the client got the email without the file and the app reported success. It can no longer be added, and if one arrives empty by another route the send stops and names it.

Security

The app's first security review, focused on what can realistically happen to a translator: a bad file you open yourself, and a web page open in your browser while Gestrics is running.

  • Restoring an incomplete backup no longer deletes issued invoices. Restoring a backup made with an older version — one that did not yet save the invoice ledger — emptied that ledger and reported success. And since invoice numbering is derived from it, the next invoice was named 2026-001 again: a number already sent to another client. The app now stops before touching anything, tells you how many issued invoices would be lost and with which numbers, and only continues if you confirm it explicitly.
  • A backup with a tampered invoice ledger is never stored. Ledger entries are hash-chained precisely so that altering one shows; but on restore they were written back as-is, unchecked. A hand-edited backup file could write invented invoices for any amount. The whole chain is now verified before anything is restored, and an entry that does not check out never reaches the disk under any circumstances. If the backup's ledger is damaged, the app tells you and offers to recover everything else - contacts, jobs, invoices - leaving the ledger empty, rather than leaving you unable to restore at all.
  • A restore that fails halfway no longer leaves your data halfway. Previously the tables were emptied and then filled row by row, skipping the ones that failed: the result was "restored" with an error count beside it, and an incomplete database. A restore is now a single operation — all of it or none of it — and if something fails it tells you which row, with your data untouched.
  • The local server only answers requests addressed to 127.0.0.1. Gestrics runs a server on your machine so the app itself can talk to it. Even though it only listened locally, a web page open in your browser could reach it by pointing its own domain at your machine, and read your contacts, invoices and stored keys from there. The app now checks which name it was called by and turns away anything that is not your own.
  • The date of the last licence check is now stored encrypted. The 7-day offline allowance hangs off it, and it sat in plain text inside license-config.json: a date in a JSON file inviting you to change it in a text editor. It is now encrypted like everything else, and a hand-written date is discarded rather than trusted. This is the same rule already applied to the trial record; this file had simply been left out. If the first launch after updating happens offline, one online check is needed to regain the offline allowance.
  • An imported contact can no longer smuggle a formula into your exports. Excel and LibreOffice execute a cell that starts with =, +, - or @. If you imported a contact list from an agency and one of its fields carried a formula, it was stored as-is: exporting your contacts and opening the file ran that formula on your machine. Those cells are now marked as text in CSV and TSV exports. Amounts are left alone, so the CSV for your accountant still adds up, and re-importing one of your own exports into Gestrics gives back the original text.
  • At 14 days the app now really does close. The end of the trial was enforced by the screen alone: behind it the local server kept answering everything, so there was still a way to go on using the app without a licence. It is now closed from the inside too, and the only thing left open is what you need in order to activate. Your data is untouched: it is all still there, and it comes back the moment you enter your key.
  • Starting a fresh data folder no longer grants another trial. The start of the trial is anchored to the oldest trace of your own work, but that was only consulted when the record was missing: starting from scratch and bringing your data over AFTERWARDS gave you fourteen new days losing nothing. It is now always consulted, and the older of the two dates wins. This also fixes the honest case: restoring a backup on a new laptop continues the same trial instead of starting another.

  • Outreach: contacts, email templates, batch sending with rate limits, per-contact status tracking and history.
  • Projects: jobs with word counts, rates, CAT analysis import, time tracking, deliverables and a per-client glossary.
  • Invoicing: invoices with VAT and withholding tax, a purpose-built PDF, a hash-chained ledger compliant with Veri*Factu, rectifications, per-country tax profiles and Stripe payment links.
  • Metrics: income, expenses, response rates and a per-client breakdown.
  • Full backups, plus contact export and import in CSV, TSV, Excel and ODS.
  • SMTP sending with automatic provider detection.
  • Spanish and English.
